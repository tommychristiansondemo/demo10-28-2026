How the AI Trade Desk picks trades

Goal: find trades you could buy now and sell at a profit by Friday. Every minute, a two-stage pipeline runs. First a transparent quantitative screen over live Schwab market data, then a large language model (Claude on Amazon Bedrock) that reads the screen's output and the overall market and makes the final call.

1 · Live data

  • Real-time quotes from Schwab for about 75 large, liquid, optionable stocks and sector ETFs, plus a market dashboard (SPY, QQQ, IWM, VIX, sector ETFs, Treasuries).
  • Three months of daily price history per symbol (refreshed once a day) for trend and volatility measures.
  • Live option chains for the November 6 expiration on the most promising names.

2 · Stock screen (bullish score)

Two short-term patterns with a long research track record in liquid US stocks, plus a few adjustments:

  • Uptrend filter: price above its 50-day average and the 20-day above the 50-day.
  • Pullback in an uptrend: a 2-day RSI below 25 means the stock dipped sharply for a day or two inside a healthy trend. Those dips tend to bounce within days (short-term mean reversion).
  • Breakout: trading within 1.5% of its 20-day high, up on the day, with volume running at least 1.2× its normal pace.
  • Relative strength: rewarded for beating SPY today, penalized for lagging it.
  • Enough movement: average daily range (ATR) of 1.5–5% earns a bonus, since it's enough to reach a target in a few sessions. Above 7% is penalized as a coin flip.
  • No chasing: names already up more than 6% today are penalized.
  • One share must fit within the $500 per-order limit.

3 · Vertical spread builder

  • Bull call spreads on the strongest names (buy a call, sell a higher-strike call). These are debit spreads: the most you can lose is what you pay.
  • Early-assignment safety: the sold call could in theory be exercised early by its owner. That is only worthwhile for them just before an ex-dividend date, so stocks with an ex-dividend date before expiration are skipped. Bear put spreads are not offered, because a sold put that goes deep in the money can be exercised early at any time.
  • The long leg is near the money (delta 0.38–0.68). The app tries each nearby short strike and keeps spreads costing 25–62% of their width and under $480 per spread.
  • Liquidity filters: both legs need a real two-sided market (bid/ask gap at most 35% of mid) and open interest of at least 25.
  • For each candidate the app estimates the probability of finishing beyond breakeven from the option market's own deltas, and computes an expected return per dollar risked. The best spread per stock is kept, and spreads are scored by the stock's setup score plus twice that expected return.

4 · Claude's review

  • The top 15 stocks and top 12 spreads, with every metric above and the market dashboard, go to Claude (Sonnet 4.6 on Amazon Bedrock). Claude is asked to rank them for the best chance of a profitable exit by Friday, weighing the market mood, sector rotation, setup quality and risk/reward, and to flag likely earnings risk.
  • For each pick Claude writes the one-line thesis you see, a target and stop for stocks, and a confidence (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW). Confidence is Claude's judgment, not a measured probability.
  • Claude's order becomes the final ranking. If the model is unavailable, the quant scores are used and the card says "Quant screen" with LOW confidence.
  • Claude re-reads the market every minute while the market is open and every 30 minutes otherwise.

5 · Your choices shape the list

  • Reject removes an idea for good, and the next-ranked idea moves up. A rejected stock never returns, and a rejected spread keeps that stock's spreads in the same direction out too, even with new strikes.
  • Accept re-checks the live price at the moment you click and sizes the order as close to $500 as possible without going over. In demo mode the limit price is set 10% below the current price, so orders usually wait unfilled.
  • Any stock the account already holds a position or open order in (shares or options) is left out, and re-checked at the moment you click. That way an option elsewhere in the account can never be assigned against shares the class bought.

This is a teaching demo of how quickly an AI-assisted trading app can be built. Its rules are simple heuristics, it has not been backtested, and nothing here is investment advice. The orders, however, are real.
